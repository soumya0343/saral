# Grace Period, Lapse and Revival

## What the grace period is

A grace period of 30 days is allowed after the premium due date for renewal. You can pay the premium during this time without the policy being treated as lapsed. The 30-day grace period applies whether you pay annually, half-yearly or monthly.

## Is the policy active during the grace period

For life products such as term life, cover continues during the grace period: if the insured event happens within the 30 days, the claim is payable after deducting the unpaid premium. For health policies, paying within the grace period keeps your continuity benefits, but hospitalization that starts before the renewal premium is received may not be payable; check your policy schedule.

## When a policy lapses

If the premium is not paid by the end of the 30-day grace period, the policy lapses. No claim is payable for an event that happens while the policy is lapsed. For a health policy, a lapse also resets accrued waiting-period benefits, such as credit towards the pre-existing disease waiting period, and the no-claim bonus is lost.

## Reviving a lapsed term life policy

A lapsed term life policy can be revived within 5 years from the due date of the first unpaid premium by paying all overdue premiums with interest. Revival may need a health declaration or medical tests (fresh underwriting), and the insurer may accept, decline or revive on changed terms. Cover restarts only after revival is approved; events during the lapse remain not payable.

## Reinstating a lapsed health policy

A lapsed health policy must be reinstated, which may require fresh underwriting. Because continuity is broken, waiting periods for pre-existing diseases and specified illnesses start again from the reinstatement date. Contact support as soon as possible if you have missed a renewal.
