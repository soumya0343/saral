# Room-Rent Limits and Proportionate Deduction

## What a room-rent limit is

Some health plan variants cap the room rent the policy pays per day, for example at 1% of the sum insured per day for a normal room and 2% per day for ICU. Your policy schedule states whether a room-rent cap applies to your policy and what the limit is. Plans without a room-rent cap pay room rent at actuals, within the sum insured.

## How proportionate deduction works

If you stay in a room whose daily rent is above your eligible room-rent cap, the insurer pays only the eligible room rent. In addition, charges that depend on the room category, such as surgeon fees, operation theatre charges and doctor visit fees, are paid in the same proportion as the eligible room rent bears to the actual room rent. This is called proportionate deduction.

## Example of proportionate deduction

Suppose your room-rent cap is ₹5,000 per day and you choose a room costing ₹8,000 per day. The eligible proportion is 5,000 divided by 8,000, which is 62.5%. For a ₹1,00,000 bill of surgeon and theatre charges, only ₹62,500 is payable and the remaining ₹37,500 is borne by you, along with the extra room rent above the cap.

## How to avoid proportionate deduction

Before admission, ask the hospital's insurance desk which room categories fit within your room-rent limit, and choose a room at or below the cap. Medicines, implants and diagnostic tests are generally not reduced proportionately. If your claim was reduced, your claim settlement letter names the clause under which the deduction was made.
