# EMI Bounce Charges and Credit Score

## What an EMI bounce is

An EMI bounce happens when your EMI auto-debit or cheque fails, usually because of insufficient funds in your account on the EMI date. Each bounce incurs a bounce charge plus penal interest on the overdue amount, as listed in the schedule of charges in your loan agreement. Your bank may also charge its own fee for the failed debit.

## What to do if your EMI bounces

If your EMI bounces, pay the overdue amount as soon as possible through your account or a payment link from support, using UPI, net banking or a debit card. Penal interest keeps adding up for every day the amount stays overdue. If you expect a temporary difficulty, contact support before the EMI date to discuss your options.

## How missed EMIs affect your credit score

Your loan repayment record is reported to credit bureaus every month. A missed or late EMI can lower your credit score, and delays of 30 days or more have a bigger impact and stay on your credit report for years. A lower credit score can make future loans and credit cards harder or costlier to get.

## Changing your EMI date to avoid bounces

If your EMI date does not match your salary date, you can change the EMI date once per loan tenure by raising a request. Keeping enough balance in your account a day before the EMI date helps avoid bounces and the charges and credit score impact that come with them.
