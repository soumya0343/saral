# Motor No-Claim Bonus (NCB) and Transfer

## What motor NCB is

The motor no-claim bonus (NCB) is a discount on the own-damage premium for every claim-free year. It does not reduce the third-party premium. The NCB grows with consecutive claim-free years: 20% after one year, 25% after two, 35% after three, 45% after four, and 50% after five or more years.

## What happens to motor NCB after a claim

If you make an own-damage claim in a policy year, the motor NCB falls to zero at the next renewal, and you start earning it again from the next claim-free year. The NCB is also lost if the motor policy is not renewed within 90 days of its expiry. An NCB protection add-on, where available, lets you keep the NCB after a limited number of claims.

## Transferring NCB to a new vehicle

The motor NCB belongs to you as the owner, not to the vehicle. When you sell your vehicle, the insurance can be transferred to the buyer, but the NCB stays with you. Ask for an NCB certificate (NCB retention letter) and submit it when insuring your new vehicle of the same class. The NCB can be used within 3 years of selling the old vehicle.

## Keeping NCB when switching insurers

You can carry your motor NCB when you renew with a different insurer. The new insurer will ask for your previous policy copy or an NCB confirmation showing that no claim was made. Declaring a wrong NCB can lead to an own-damage claim being rejected, so share the correct claim history.
