# Pre-Existing Disease Waiting Period

## What counts as a pre-existing disease

A pre-existing disease (PED) is any condition, illness or injury that you had, or were diagnosed with or treated for, in the 48 months before your health policy started. Common examples are diabetes, high blood pressure, thyroid disorders and asthma. Pre-existing diseases must be declared in the proposal form when you buy the policy.

## How long the pre-existing disease waiting period is

Under our standard health plan, pre-existing diseases are covered after a waiting period of 36 months of continuous coverage. Hospitalization related to a declared pre-existing disease during those 36 months is not payable. After the waiting period is completed, claims for that condition are paid like any other covered illness. Some plan variants print a different PED waiting period in the policy schedule, and the schedule of your own policy decides.

## Other waiting periods in health insurance

Apart from the pre-existing disease waiting period, an initial waiting period of 30 days applies from the policy start date, during which only accidental hospitalization is covered. Specified illnesses such as cataract, hernia and joint replacement have a waiting period of 24 months. Hospitalization due to an accident is covered from the first day.

## Keeping your waiting-period credit

The pre-existing disease waiting period counts only continuous coverage. If you renew within the 30-day grace period, the time already served carries forward. If the policy lapses, accrued waiting-period benefits are reset. When you port a policy to us from another insurer, the waiting period already served there is credited up to the previous sum insured.

## Not declaring a pre-existing disease

If a pre-existing disease was not declared in the proposal form, a claim related to it may be rejected and the policy may be cancelled. If you forgot to declare a condition, contact support as soon as possible to update your health declaration.
