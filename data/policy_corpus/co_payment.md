# Co-Payment and Deductible

## What co-payment means

Co-payment, or co-pay, is a fixed percentage of every admissible health claim that you pay yourself, while the insurer pays the rest. For example, with a 10% co-pay on an admissible claim of ₹50,000, you pay ₹5,000 and the insurer pays ₹45,000. Co-pay is calculated on the admissible amount, after other deductions such as non-payable items and any room-rent proportionate deduction.

## When co-payment applies

Not every health policy has a co-pay. A co-payment applies only if your policy schedule says so, which is common in some plan variants and in plans for senior citizens. The schedule states the co-pay percentage and whether it applies to all claims or only to certain treatments.

## Co-payment vs deductible

A co-pay is a percentage of each claim. A deductible is a fixed amount you bear first in a policy year before the policy starts paying. Our standard health plan has no deductible unless one is stated in your policy schedule. Both a co-pay and a deductible lower the premium because you share part of the cost.

## Can co-payment be removed

Co-payment is part of your plan variant, so it cannot be removed in the middle of a policy year. At renewal, you can ask to move to a variant without co-pay, which usually has a higher premium and may need fresh underwriting.
