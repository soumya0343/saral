# Day-Care Procedures

## What a day-care procedure is

A day-care procedure is a treatment or surgery that needs admission to a hospital or day-care centre but is completed in less than 24 hours because of modern technology. Normally, our health policy covers hospitalization only when the admitted stay is 24 hours or more; day-care procedures are the exception and are covered without a 24-hour stay.

## Which day-care procedures are covered

Covered day-care procedures are listed in your policy schedule. Common examples include cataract surgery, dialysis, chemotherapy, radiotherapy, lithotripsy for kidney stones and tonsillectomy. If a procedure is not on the day-care list, it is covered only when it needs an admitted stay of 24 hours or more.

## Waiting periods and claims for day-care treatment

Day-care procedures follow the same waiting periods as other treatment. For example, cataract surgery is a specified illness with a waiting period of 24 months, even though it is done as day care. Day-care claims can be cashless at a network hospital or reimbursed, and related pre-hospitalization expenses up to 30 days and post-hospitalization expenses up to 60 days are also payable.

## Day-care vs outpatient (OPD) treatment

Outpatient (OPD) treatment, such as a doctor consultation, tests done without admission, or medicines bought for use at home, is not a day-care procedure. OPD expenses are not covered under the base health plan. If you are unsure whether a planned treatment counts as day care, ask support or the hospital's insurance desk before the procedure.
